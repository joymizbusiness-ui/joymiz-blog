# How to Write a CV in 2026: The Complete Guide

Your CV is often the first impression a hiring manager has of you. In a competitive job market, a well-crafted CV can be the difference between landing an interview and being overlooked. But with evolving hiring practices, ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) scanning your document, and recruiters spending an average of just seven seconds on an initial review, writing a CV that works is more important than ever.

This guide walks you through everything you need to know about writing a CV in 2026 — from structure and content to formatting and common mistakes.

---

## What Is a CV and Why Does It Matter?

A CV (Curriculum Vitae) is a document that summarizes your professional history, skills, education, and achievements. Unlike a resume, which is typically one page, a CV can be longer depending on your experience level and the role you are applying for.

In 2026, most companies use ATS software to filter applications before a human ever sees them. This means your CV needs to be both human-readable and machine-friendly. A poorly formatted CV can be automatically rejected, no matter how qualified you are.

---

## CV Structure: What to Include

A strong CV follows a clear structure. Here is the standard order that works across industries:

### 1. Contact Information

At the top of your CV, include:

- Full name
- Phone number
- Professional email address
- LinkedIn profile URL
- Portfolio or website (if relevant)

Avoid using unprofessional email addresses. If your current email is something like `partyguy99@email.com`, create a new one with your name.

### 2. Personal Profile or Personal Statement

This is a short paragraph at the top of your CV that summarizes who you are professionally. Think of it as your elevator pitch. It should be two to four sentences and tailored to the specific job you are applying for.

**Strong example:**
> Detail-oriented marketing professional with five years of experience in digital campaign management and content strategy. Proven track record of increasing organic traffic by 140% and managing six-figure ad budgets. Seeking a senior marketing role where I can drive brand growth.

**Weak example:**
> Hard-working person looking for a job where I can use my skills and grow as a professional.

The difference is specific. Strong CVs use numbers, job titles, and concrete achievements.

### 3. Work Experience

List your work experience in reverse chronological order (most recent first). For each role, include:

- Job title
- Company name and location
- Dates of employment
- Four to six bullet points describing your responsibilities and achievements

Each bullet point should start with a strong action verb and include quantifiable results where possible.

**Instead of:** "Responsible for managing social media accounts"
**Write:** "Managed five social media accounts across three platforms, growing combined followers by 35% in six months"

### 4. Education

Include your most recent and relevant education. For each entry:

- Degree and field of study
- Institution name
- Graduation date (or expected graduation date)
- Relevant coursework, honors, or GPA (if notable)

If you have been working for several years, your education section can be brief. If you are a recent graduate, you can expand this section with relevant projects, thesis work, or academic achievements.

### 5. Skills

List skills that are directly relevant to the job. Group them into categories if you have a wide range:

- **Technical skills:** Programming languages, software, tools
- **Languages:** Spoken or written proficiency
- **Soft skills:** Leadership, communication, problem-solving

Be honest. Listing skills you do not have will backfire during interviews or on the job.

### 6. Additional Sections (Optional)

Depending on your background, you might also include:

- Certifications and training
- Volunteer experience
- Publications or presentations
- Awards and honors
- Professional affiliations

Only include sections that add value to your application.

## Formatting Tips That Make a Difference

A great CV with poor formatting will still get rejected. Here are formatting rules to follow:

- **Use a clean, professional font** like Calibri, Arial, or Helvetica in 10 to 12 point size
- **Keep margins between 0.5 and 1 inch** on all sides
- **Use consistent formatting** for headings, dates, and bullet points throughout
- **Save as PDF** unless the employer specifically requests a different format
- **Name your file professionally:** `FirstName_LastName_CV.pdf` is ideal

For most professionals, a one-page CV is sufficient. If you have ten-plus years of highly relevant experience, two pages is acceptable. Never exceed two pages unless you are in academia or a senior executive.

## Tailoring Your CV for Each Application

One of the biggest mistakes job seekers make is sending the same CV to every job. Each job description contains specific keywords and requirements. Your CV should mirror those.

Here is a simple process:

1. Read the job description carefully
2. Identify the key skills and qualifications they mention
3. Adjust your personal profile to reflect those priorities
4. Reorder your bullet points so the most relevant experience comes first
5. Incorporate the exact language they use (if it genuinely applies to you)

This is not about being dishonest. It is about presenting your existing experience in the way that matters most to that particular employer.

## Common CV Mistakes to Avoid

Even small mistakes can cost you an interview. Watch out for these:

- **Spelling and grammar errors.** Always proofread. Ask a friend to review your CV before you send it.
- **Using an objective statement instead of a profile.** Objectives like "Seeking a challenging position" are outdated. A personal profile is more effective.
- **Listing duties instead of achievements.** Everyone knows what a project manager does. Show what you specifically accomplished.
- **Including irrelevant information.** Your CV does not need your marital status, photo (in most countries), or every job you have ever had.
- **Using a generic template without customization.** Templates are a starting point, not a final product.

## Final Checklist Before You Send

Before submitting your CV, run through this checklist:

- [ ] Is your contact information current and professional?
- [ ] Does your personal profile directly address the role you are applying for?
- [ ] Are your bullet points achievement-focused with measurable results?
- [ ] Have you removed irrelevant experience and information?
- [ ] Is the formatting consistent throughout?
- [ ] Have you proofread for spelling and grammar?
- [ ] Is the file saved as a PDF with a professional file name?
