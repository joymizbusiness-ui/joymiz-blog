# 7 CV Mistakes That Are Costing You Interviews

You are qualified for the role. You have the experience, the skills, and the motivation. But your CV is not getting you interviews. The problem is not you — it is your CV.

Recruiters and hiring managers make quick decisions. Studies show that the average recruiter spends about seven seconds on an initial CV scan. In that time, they decide whether to keep reading or move on. If your CV contains common mistakes, it gets discarded before anyone sees how great you are.

Here are seven CV mistakes that are likely costing you interviews, along with practical fixes you can apply today.

---

## Mistake 1: Sending the Same CV to Every Job

This is the most common and most damaging mistake. A generic CV that is not tailored to the specific role tells the recruiter one thing: you did not put in the effort.

Every job description is different. They emphasize different skills, use different language, and prioritize different experience. If your CV does not reflect those differences, it looks like every other generic application in the pile.

**How to fix it:**

Read each job description carefully. Identify the key skills and qualifications they mention. Then adjust your personal profile, reorder your experience bullet points, and use their language to describe your relevant skills. This does not mean rewriting your CV from scratch every time. It means making targeted adjustments so your CV speaks directly to that role.

---

## Mistake 2: Focusing on Duties Instead of Achievements

Listing what you were responsible for does not tell anyone how well you did it. Every project manager manages projects. Every salesperson sells products. What sets you apart is the result you achieved.

Compare these two bullet points:

- "Responsible for managing social media accounts"
- "Managed five social media accounts, increasing engagement by 45% over six months"

The first one describes a duty. The second one describes an achievement. The second one is far more compelling.

**How to fix it:**

For every role, ask yourself: What did I accomplish? What improved because of my work? What specific results can I point to? Use numbers, percentages, and concrete outcomes wherever possible. If you do not have exact figures, provide reasonable estimates and label them as such.

---

## Mistake 3: Typos and Grammatical Errors

A single typo might seem harmless. But to a recruiter, it signals carelessness. If you cannot proofread a two-page document that represents your professional reputation, what does that say about your attention to detail on the job?

Spelling errors, grammatical mistakes, and inconsistent formatting all fall into this category. They are small individually but collectively they undermine your credibility.

**How to fix it:**

After writing your CV, proofread it at least twice. Read it out loud to catch awkward phrasing. Use a tool like Grammarly or a browser spell checker. Then ask a friend or colleague to review it. A fresh pair of eyes catches mistakes you have become blind to.

---

## Mistake 4: Including Irrelevant Information

Your CV is not a autobiography. Every line should serve a purpose: to convince the reader that you are the right person for this specific job. Irrelevant information wastes space and dilutes your message.

Common examples include:

- Marital status or date of birth (in most countries, this is not required and can lead to bias)
- A photo (unless you are in a country or industry where it is standard)
- Every job you have ever held (only include relevant experience)
- Hobbies that do not relate to the role
- References available upon request (this is assumed and takes up space)

**How to fix it:**

Review every section of your CV and ask: Does this directly support my application for this role? If the answer is no, remove it or minimize it. Save space for the information that matters most.

---

## Mistake 5: Poor Formatting and Layout

You might have incredible experience, but if your CV is hard to read, recruiters will not spend the time to find it. Poor formatting includes:

- Walls of text with no bullet points or section breaks
- Inconsistent fonts, sizes, or spacing
- Tiny margins that make the page look cramped
- Using a font that is too decorative or hard to read
- Not using headings to organize sections

ATS software also struggles with poorly formatted CVs. If the system cannot parse your document correctly, your information may not even make it into the recruiter's view.

**How to fix it:**

Use a clean, professional layout. Stick to one or two fonts at most. Use headings, bullet points, and white space to break up text. Keep margins between 0.5 and 1 inch. ## Mistake 6: Vague or Generic Language

Phrases like "hard-working team player," "excellent communication skills," and "results-driven professional" appear on millions of CVs. They mean nothing because everyone says them. They are filler that takes up space without providing evidence.

Recruiters want to see specific examples that prove you have these qualities, not just claims that you do.

**How to fix it:**

Replace generic claims with specific examples. Instead of saying you have "excellent communication skills," describe a situation where you used those skills:

- "Presented quarterly performance reports to a board of 12 executives, receiving positive feedback on clarity and data visualization"
- "Wrote and published 20 blog posts that generated over 50,000 combined page views"

Show, do not tell.

## Mistake 7: Missing Contact Information or Using Unprofessional Details

This sounds obvious, but it happens more often than you think. Some CVs are missing a phone number, email address, or both. Others use email addresses like `coolguy99@email.com` or `princess2005@email.com`. These details immediately undermine your professionalism.

Your contact section is the first thing a recruiter looks for when they want to invite you for an interview. If it is missing or unprofessional, you have created an unnecessary barrier.

**How to fix it:**

At the top of your CV, include:

- Your full name
- A professional email address (firstname.lastname@email.com is ideal)
- A phone number you actually answer
- Your LinkedIn profile URL
- A portfolio or website link (if relevant)

If your current email is unprofessional, create a new one. It takes two minutes and eliminates a red flag from your application.

---

## Bonus Mistake: Not Having a CV at All

Some people rely solely on online applications and networking, assuming their experience speaks for itself. But almost every application requires a CV. Not having one ready means you are scrambling at the last minute, which leads to all of the mistakes above.

## How to Fix Your CV Today

The good news is that every one of these mistakes is fixable. You do not need to start from scratch. Here is a quick action plan:

1. **Read your CV out loud.** You will immediately catch awkward phrasing and filler.
2. **Check for achievements, not duties.** Rewrite at least three bullet points with measurable results.
3. **Proofread twice.** Then have someone else proofread it.
4. **Remove everything irrelevant.** Be ruthless. Every line earns its place.
5. **Check your formatting.** Clean, consistent, and easy to scan.
6. **Update your contact information.** Make sure it is professional and complete.
